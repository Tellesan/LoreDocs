# Erdana: Reference Guide

## Premise

- **Erdana** is the world and also the goddess of life the faithful worship. Almost nobody knows the world is her body.
- She is dying slowly, with centuries left. Her life, the ember, leaks out of the world faster with every use. Whole regions are already gray waste that drains anything crossing it.
- **Wylter the Eternal**, the Emperor, has drunk from her directly at the Heart for two hundred years. He calls it just rule and the Church calls it her blessing. His drinking has shrunk millennia of decline to centuries and made him deathless.
- At the center of the setting: a beautiful lie; a slandered remnant (the Old Vigil) guilty of nearly everything except killing the world; and a war that ends the world if anyone wins it the obvious way.

---

## Ember

- **Ember** is the world's life, in soil, air, and the green, and above all in living things. Loose ember in the open world thins every year.
- Common folk and the Vigil call it **ember**. The Church calls it **Grace**, which priests "channel" rather than spend. The old Vigil called it the **Wellspring**, an endless source; survivors won't say that word now.
- **Gleam** is ember's faint light, visible to those with the sight. Living land burns, dying edges flicker, deadzones are dark. Stolen ember makes a person blaze; a spent master barely shows; a corpse in the grays is black.
- The **Kindled** are anyone born able to feel and move ember. Most are never trained and hide it. The Vigil trains the ones it finds as **Tenders**, who spend themselves carefully and carry their dead. The Church burns the ones it takes as fuel, the **Kindling**. Most are found by no one.

## The Ladder

Using ember always costs ember. There are three ways to pay, each harder to reach and each stronger because someone else pays more.

1. **Spending yourself.** Open to any Kindled. You age wrong and hollow out, growing quiet and strange. The kindest end up here, and it kills them over years.
2. **Taking from others.** Skimming ember off the living nearby or the freshly dead. Free, far stronger, and it must be climbed to. Crossing this line makes you **Turned**. Skimming leaves people gray and tired; emptying them is the Witherer's art.
3. **Drinking from the world.** Pulling straight from the land. Almost nobody reaches it. It feels harmless, but the supply is Erdana, and every sip hastens her end.

The Emperor stands at the top of this ladder, doing the ordinary thing at its limit.

Every use lets a little of Erdana in. Old masters carry a grief not their own and grow still and distant. Their famous calm comes from being mostly spent and full of a god's sorrow.

## Disciplines

- Using ember means narrowing to one grip, which reshapes your ember into a fixed channel. A second, very different groove fights the first. Most master one discipline, or two similar ones learned young. Three is legendary and usually shallow. A true master couldn't learn another.
- Disciplines are named for the practitioner. Any discipline can run on any rung of the ladder, so one school can produce a saint and a monster.

#### Quickener
Burns ember into the body for speed, strength, and reflexes. From inside, the world slows; from outside, someone simply moves too fast and too precisely. Self-fueled, it burns practitioners out young, cold-fingered by forty. A Turned Quickener grays everyone within thirty feet to feed himself.

#### Hand
Moves things by gripping their thread of ember, never by pushing. Objects come reluctantly, and heavy work leaves a drained chill in the air and the object.

#### Voice
Works the ember inside another person: suggestion and calming at a light touch, compulsion with a heavy hand, taking at its deepest. It feels like reaching into a warm sealed thing. Voices are feared, and many grow lonely, having lost the sense that people are closed off from them.

#### Mender
Heals by pouring ember into wounds. Self-spending Menders age with every life saved. Taking Menders can save a patient by draining whoever holds his hand. World-drinking Menders heal at no visible cost, are loved and called holy, and push the god closer to death each time. The same grip reversed pulls ember out.

#### Witherer
That reversed grip as a weapon. A Witherer pulls a living thing's ember out by hand until nothing is left. It takes contact and time and kills like drowning, holding on through the struggle. A brush leaves a victim grayed, aged, and missing a piece. Each emptying floods the killer with the victim's terror and grief, which builds up and drives Witherers strange. Strong targets resist, so a Witherer is an apocalypse to the defenseless and a hard duel for a master. Few will cut this groove into a child; nearly all Witherers come from Turned lineages or from the Church, which makes them only as Sentinels. Gentle Menders are watched for the day they flip the grip.

#### Shaper
Changes what a thing is by working its ember: drawing it out as heat makes fire, loosening it makes metal run and re-set. Only Shapers make soulsteel, which puts them at the center of the Vigil.

#### Listener
The seers. Opening to ember means hearing Erdana, the grief and terror of an enormous dying mind without language. Listeners return with fragments, dread, and certainties they can't source. Deep ones go inwardly blind and start speaking in its rhythm. None realize it's one voice, or that it's begging.

#### Tide-reader
Feels ember flowing, pooling, and draining like currents against the skin. A modest trade: finding living land, safe paths through deadzones, and where the green is shrinking. Everyone knows the land is greenest near Abira, and the faith calls that holiness. A Tide-reader who compared the flow across many distant places could feel what nobody sees: the ember itself running inward everywhere, like water toward a drain, toward the holiest site in the world.

#### Pairings
Quickener and Hand reach outward (the warrior's pair). Voice and Mender reach into others (a pair the Vigil always watched). Listener and Tide-reader are receptive; someone with both could feel the inward flow and faintly hear what cries at its center, making them the likeliest person to find the truth. Such people are vanishingly rare.

## Soulsteel and Vessels

- **Soulsteel** is steel a Shaper has worked to hold ember. Ordinary metal lets it run out or drains the person pouring. Anything soulsteel is a **vessel**, named for its purpose.
- It looks like fine near-chrome metal and takes treatment and color like steel. Charge shows as gleam: faint to Tide-readers, clearer to any Kindled, visible to anyone when full and working. A drawn vigil-blade lights its edge; a Sentinel at full burn glows like a forge. Brightness shows what's left.
- Each piece is handmade, slowly, and soulsteel has never been common. Even at the Vigil's height there was just enough for every Tender's blade and every cell's vessels. Now a few hunted Shapers can't supply even that, and blades are guarded heirlooms. A cell without a Shaper lives off what it has. The Church calls soulsteel witch-steel and hunts Shapers above all Kindled.

### Charge

- Vessels never leak. Ember stays until spent, so ancient vessels can still glow and buried blades can still hold their masters.
- Finer work fills slower. A vigil-blade takes an hour or two of careful, calm channeling; a crossing-core takes ember as fast as it's poured. Spending is always faster than filling, so a full vessel is hours of work that can go in seconds. Fine vessels can't be filled by violently ripping ember from someone; crude ones take anything.
- Tenders refill their blades from their own ember after a fight, and can't if they're too drained.
- A borrowed vessel is hard to charge at first but eases as you leave your ember in it, until it answers like your own. That's how heirs inherit blades, and why stolen ones stay hostile.
- Ember can come from your own life, a living person drained into the vessel, the freshly dead before their ember scatters, or the world (rare, slower, harder). Denser sources like the closer-to-god peoples hold more. Only the morality differs: a Sentinel draining a prisoner into his armor and a Tender charging a blade from themselves perform the same act.
- Charge can't pass between vessels; it leaves only by being spent in use, so it can't be stockpiled or traded. A living Kindled must always do the filling, whatever ember goes in. The exception is a **battery**, which stores ember and feeds other vessels. Batteries are rare and nearly all belong to the Empire and Church, which need to move charge where their people can't go.

### Vigil-Blades

- A Tender's soulsteel sword, earned at the end of apprenticeship and carried for life.
- It cuts by pulling ember out along its edge so fast that flesh, iron, or stone gives way. Wounds are gray, frostbitten, and bloodless, a hair-thin strip of desert in living meat, and frost forms around the blade. In the dark it gleams faintly, and sensitives feel pressure, cold, and someone grieving.
- **Seeding** is the coming-of-age rite. A Shaper forges a new blade or, more often now, prepares an old one whose last master has been spent. The apprentice pours in their own ember. Too little and it stays dead; too much and it drinks enough to hollow or kill them. Passing means knowing your own depth. The blade becomes partly you, keyed to you without taming.
- At first it runs on you; each fight costs ember, repaid by refilling. A blade won't spend its charge for a hand it doesn't know, so a stranger who swings it pays from their own body as cold climbs their arm. Stolen blades kill as often as they help, so blades stay in the line.
- Decades later a dying master pours all their ember into the student's blade. The blade then runs on the master, the student no longer pays to use it, and the master is present in the steel, since stored ember carries its source. Over the years the student spends the master down until the blade goes quiet and must be filled from the wielder again in old age. That student dies into their own apprentice's blade, and the empty one goes back to a Shaper for someone new. Each generation's death pays for the next one's prime.

#### Wraith-blades
- The same craft fueled by a death a Witherer forces into the steel. What wakes is torn, terrified, and furious. It gives power but fights the hand and pushes toward killing, the death still trying to finish.
- It burns hot and fast and runs down far quicker than a vigil-blade, so the Turned must keep feeding it killings.
- Only a Listener touching both can tell the two apart. Old vigil-blades turn up full and ready; old wraith-blades turn up burnt out, starving, and hostile.

### Resting-Stones

- A Tender can die into a **resting-stone** instead of a blade. A blade keeps a master to fight; a stone keeps one to teach.
- It's the Vigil's library with a person for a book. Feeding it ember wakes it, and each conversation spends a little of the master. A fresh stone is nearly the person; an old one gives shorter answers; a spent one is a teacher talked to death.
- The more knowledge is needed, the faster its holder is lost. A cell might have to spend the only Shaper-stone that remembers a rite, or leave it quiet and let the knowledge die with the living.

### Other Vessels

- All vessels are serious Shaper work, rare and valuable.
- A **crossing-core** is a crude vessel that bleeds charge into the grays so the deadzone drinks from it instead of from people. It charges fast from any source and is a guarded, irreplaceable asset for caravans and graywalker crews.
- **Sentinel-armor** is a full soulsteel suit worn as a reservoir, finer than a core and cruder than a blade. It's charged ahead of time and is the Sentinel's weak point when empty.
- A **bottled discipline** is a crude discipline built into a vessel, like a force-vessel that breaks doors or one that burns. Each costs a lot of ember for a few uses, so they're saved for need. Only blunt outward disciplines bottle well; Mending and the Voice need a living hand.
- Anyone can use a vessel, which lets smugglers work without a mage. The rare talent is making and charging. With too few Shapers for vessel-armed armies, the Empire concentrates its supply in a few Sentinels and cores. An old vessel found in the dark is worth more than gold.
- The willing dead can also be bound into wardstones guarding a cell's meeting place or set into doorways as guardians.

## The Old Vigil

- In an older, civilized age, the **Ember Vigil** drew freely on a world it thought endless, called ember the Wellspring, and built its greatness on it. Its members were Tenders, keepers of the ember. When the sharpest learned that using ember drains something dying, their stewardship became a deathbed watch, and tending came to mean tending the dying and their bound dead.
- The discovery split them. Some swore never to take from anything but themselves and dwindled. Others decided to drink since the world was dying anyway, and grew strong and terrible; the Turned descend from them. Neither side is clean.
- The Emperor blamed them for everything, and they did spend the world, only on a far smaller scale. Nobody hears that distinction while the man draining the world points at them.
- No order remains, only the **Old Vigil**, said like "the old country": one person who knows and one who's learning.

### How a Tender Lives

- Master and apprentice travel together for years. The apprentice trains on the master's blade, and once the grandmaster has died into it, trains with the grandmaster too, sometimes feeling them arrive mid-training.
- When the apprentice comes of age and seeds their own blade, the master is usually too spent to travel. They part, and the master's last instruction is to find an apprentice.
- Near death, the cell gathers and the master dies into the apprentice's blade with the apprentice's hand on the quench. It's love and killing at once and marks whoever does it. The master's own blade, its grandmaster long spent, goes to the cell's Shaper for a future apprentice.

### Cells

- The Vigil scatters to survive. A **cell** forms around a Shaper, the only one who can forge vessels and perform bindings. Members spread out for years, learning their disciplines and hunting lost techniques, then come home.
- A gathering is reunion, school, and funeral at once. It's the only place a hunted Tender is known; knowledge is pooled, argued, and carried away again, so the cell loses a branch rather than the tree when someone dies; and bindings happen there.
- It's also when a cell is most exposed. They never meet in the same place twice, pass the location in ways that can't be tortured out of anyone who doesn't know it yet, and assume betrayal.
- The worst fear is losing the Shaper, since nobody can bind the binder. Without a successor a cell can only pass on existing vessels and slowly goes sterile. A Shaper with no successor may cling on by drinking from the world or worse, and slowly go bad.
- Knowledge lives in heads and stones, with no authority over it, so lineages drift. Cells from one ancestor may now practice nothing alike, and meetings between them either teach or split over whose rite is right.

## The Turned

- The Vigil's grieving name for those who turned the grip and take, every one of them once an ordinary Kindled, maybe even a Tender. Also called **the Faithless**. The Church has more demonic names.
- To most people they're the worst the Kindled can become, and they're real, which suits the Emperor. He hunts them toward extinction mainly because their power is his kind; they alone might recognize him or see a brother in a Sentinel. He holds them up as proof of what the Kindled are, and the gentle Tenders, the only people who know the world is being killed, are hunted alongside them.

---

## Erdana's Light

- **Erdana's Light**, the state faith, worships Erdana as the goddess of life, a radiant presence who blesses the living and watches over creation. People once understood the goddess and the world as one, but that faded long before the Empire. "Erdana" now means both the goddess and the everyday word for the ground, and nobody saying it knows they're standing on their god.
- The holy radiance they revere is the glow of a god bleeding out.
- The Church teaches that Wylter never dies because he is just and Erdana smiles on him. The longer he lives, the holier he seems. The fact that should expose him is the proof of his sanctity, and doubting it means doubting her blessing, which is heresy. In truth he steals his life from her, and the blessed light is her ember bleeding into him. "Erdana smiles on Wylter" is taught to every child.
- Anyone who learns the truth and says it sounds like every other Kindled heretic.

## The Sentinels

- The Church's enforcers and the Vigil's hunters. **Sentinel** is the holy title. Those who know call them **the Wrought**, made things built from stolen ember. A villager says a Sentinel rode in; someone who knows says the Wrought are here.
- Each is a seven-foot man in full heavy enchanted armor, grown on other people's ember. His channel is pried open until he can drink from the world, a slow trickle compared to what he burns. Natural world-drinkers get there after a lifetime; Sentinels are forced open as children.
- A Sentinel is a Quickener and a Witherer raised on purpose. The faith that calls the Kindled **Eaters** builds its holiest soldiers from that same sin, which is part of why the Turned are hunted so hard: a captured Turned is dangerous in a fight and worse if he talks.
- Sentinels burn ember faster than the world refills them, so the Church feeds them. The Kindling, its own mages, pour their ember in as a divine gift, aging and dying young and honored for it. When that runs short, the condemned are drained as their execution. Captured Tenders are spent into their judges, and a veteran Sentinel carries a graveyard of everyone he has sentenced.
- His armor is a worn vessel. Out of it he looks too big and somehow hollow. Pre-charged plate lets him fight at full strength in bursts without a mage, and it's also his weakness: without charged plate he's just a strong, strange giant. Beat one by catching him cold, making him burn through his reservoir, then fighting what's left.
- His verdicts count as Grace-given insight and can't be appealed, so one man can roam a continent killing beyond question. His authority comes from the Emperor.

### The Making

- Sentinels are made from Kindled children over a whole childhood, taken young before the channel sets. Some parents hand them over believing it's an honor or a mercy; others sell them or have them seized. The theft of the life the child would have had is the foundation of everything after.
- Ember-scars are cut and deepened as the child grows to widen the channel, and glow dull orange under the skin for life. The child is fed ember until the body is superhuman.
- The Church cuts two grooves into every child, the Quickener's and the Witherer's. Quickening makes the most of the body; Withering lets a Sentinel close on a mage, drain them by hand, and burn the ember himself. The grips are nothing alike, and forcing both is part of why some children die.
- Training is built around the enhanced body, armed and unarmed, against one foe or many, and above all against the Kindled and the horrors of the dying world.
- Children are raised in cohorts. Some die in the making; the rest become their chapter, the closest thing to family they'll have.
- The mental shaping holds it all together and is the likeliest part to break. Each child is raised in total certainty of divine authority and his own righteousness, built before he can resist it. When a Sentinel comes loose, through a fallen chapter or a cracked faith, the whole structure collapses and the betrayal goes as deep as the conditioning.
- The making takes his childhood, body, and mind. His reward is to be a tool the Church aims and spends.

### Hunter Teams

- A Sentinel can't investigate or read a room, so he never travels alone. A team is one Sentinel plus support, usually a mage and a servant.
- The Sentinel is the judgment and the muscle.
- The mage is often a Mender (his body needs tending), sometimes a Voice to get people talking and calm towns, or a Listener to sense what's hidden. The mage does the talking in rooms that fall silent when the Sentinel ducks through the door, and can feed him ember in a pinch. The same hands may heal a village by day and help drain its hidden Tender by night.
- The servant scouts, forages, and passes for nobody in taverns and markets. Servants fight well, often find the target first, and are often the most dangerous of the three because nobody fears them.
- To humanize or complicate a team, work through the mage or servant, who see what the Sentinel is and stay, or slowly find they can't.

## Geography

### Deadzones

- A **deadzone** is land Erdana has withdrawn from. Its ember has drained or been drawn toward the center, leaving matter without what the world runs on, gangrene on a god. Graywalkers call them the **grays** or **graylands**; those who know what Erdana is call them **scars**.
- A deadzone pulls ember steadily from anything inside, drinking a traveler to a husk before thirst or heat could. Narrow ones can be crossed by the fast and strong. Wide ones can't be crossed by ordinary means, and they keep the living regions apart.
- With no life left to rot them, the dead lie preserved where they fell: husk-trees standing, travelers mummified mid-failure. There's no sound. To a sensitive it's a hole in the world, their sense gone blind and cold as their ember wicks away.

### Crossing

- Crossing is a craft, and its practitioners are valuable and disreputable.
- A crossing-core pays the land's toll while the party huddles around it, racing its fading glow against the distance, personal vessels held in reserve.
- A Tide-reader leads, because the ember withdrew unevenly, leaving threads of life and hollows that drain a person in minutes. Routes are guarded and killed over, and they go stale within about ten years as the grays spread. A graywalker's routes are worth more than their vessels.
- The deepest crossings may need other people's ember, so predatory guides drain their clients or bring the condemned and trafficked as fuel.
- Most Tide-readers end up guiding crossings and reading the flow all over the world, so the underworld is full of the minds likeliest to find the secret. The one who does might be an old graywalker who noticed, after forty years, that everything drains toward one place.

### Shape of the World

- Erdana pulls her ember toward the Heart and abandons the edges, so the world dies from the outside in. The grays follow the terrain: waste pools in flat basins, highlands stay green longer, and living rivers run like green threads into dying country.
- **The held core** is Abira and the deep green around it. Control is total and easy, with little waste to hinder travel. Law is enforced, the Church is everywhere, Sentinels are stationed there, and loyalty is sincere because nothing has tested it.
- **The throttled middle** is where the green frays. Authority arrives in waves of tax collectors, garrisons, and passing Sentinels, and the regions half rule themselves between. It's the worst place to live, with full oppression and none of the core's easy obedience. People comply perfectly and feel none of it, which makes the middle the resistance's best ground.
- **The edge** is far country cut off by the great grays, where the Empire exists on paper. Some places are lawless, run by whoever is strong or trusted. Some are resistance ground, where the Old Vigil lives openly and oppressed communities govern themselves, too far to be worth crushing yet. Others are fiefdoms, where a lord rules by his own law under imperial colors and calls it the Emperor's will, often looking more loyal than the core. The Emperor hasn't heard of half of them.
- The edge sees the Empire only when a Sentinel team crosses. While it stays, the Empire is real and absolute; then it's a name again.
- Church caravans cross with cores charged by the Kindling and condemned prisoners drained into the grays: mages spending themselves at the front, the condemned spent at the back, Sentinels untouched in the middle. The cost in lives keeps crossings rare. Smugglers cross cheaply and often, and in the far green their networks rival the Empire's.

### The Sea

- The sea isn't Erdana, so it isn't dying. It's alive in a way unrelated to ember and neither drains nor glows. To a Tide-reader it's a blank past the waterline, and Kindled at sea can spend what they carry but draw on nothing.
- It's no escape, since nobody can live on the water and the land still dies. It connects where the grays divide: linking coasts, carrying ships around dead expanses, and softening the deadening along the shore so coasts stay greener. Shipping competes with gray routes, and port cities are looser and harder to watch. The sea is the only direction in which the world isn't closing in. Its deepest waters hold a mystery of a different kind.

### Abira, the Heart

- **Abira** sits in the most alive country in the world. All ember runs toward it on the way to the Heart, so the land stays soaked while Erdana's core beneath is hollowed out. Rivers run full in the worst summers, the soil is so black that pilgrims call it holy, and gardens must be cut back constantly.
- Its rings, from the center out:
  - The **Hallowseat**, the Emperor's citadel, on the holiest ground. It's the most defended place in the world and needs it least, since everyone's faith protects it.
  - The sacred core of temples, seminaries, and high clergy, closed off more tightly toward the center.
  - The pilgrim districts, crowded, prosperous, and a bit predatory, where strangers vanish easily.
  - The high districts, where old families play loyalty politics for the highest stakes and closeness to the Hallowseat is the only currency.
  - The working city of crafts, markets, and river quarters, well off and closely watched.
  - The enclaves, home to diaspora peoples, the poor, and the marginal, watched from outside but close-knit within. Informers are weakest here, so contraband and fugitives collect.
  - The gates, where the world's traffic arrives before being sorted inward.
- Abira is the richest, most beautiful, and holiest city in the world, and it works. The green is real and the faith is sincere.

## Peoples

- The faith ranks humans first as Erdana's rightful children and other peoples as lesser, treating their suffering on the rim as proof. Non-humans gather in watched enclaves and undercities, which shelter the Old Vigil, smugglers, and enemies of the throne.
- As the land dies inward, peoples have migrated inward for centuries, unknowingly toward what's eating their homelands.
- **Diaspora** peoples fled rim homelands that are already dead. Their young have never seen the green their elders grieve.
- **Frontier** peoples still live in the outer green, proud and whole, cut off by the dead seas and living on borrowed time.
- **Margin** peoples are adapted to low ember and live where others can't. Despised and indispensable as graywalkers, they breed a powerful, resentful underworld.
- **Closer-to-god** peoples are made of denser or stranger ember. The Church calls them holy (used as sacred material) or abominable (hunted). Their denser ember puts them near the central secret: Sentinels prize them for draining, and the Emperor may want them.

## The Resistance

- The Empire breeds its own opposition. Everyone watches everyone, denouncing a neighbor proves loyalty, and people perform loyalty all day to survive. The resistance lives in the gap between shown and felt loyalty.
- It stays splintered on purpose. Cells don't know each other, so none can betray the others. Nothing can grow into a movement, but small things pass down the line: warnings, caravan routes, names to avoid. Smugglers carry these whispers on their routes through the grays.
- Tenders are the network's connecting thread. Hunted and always moving, a Tender circulates among several groups as mentor, courier, and living library, never naming one to another and leaving before drawing a team. If a Tender falls, the groups are orphaned but not exposed.
- Nobody knows the truth, so nobody knows what winning means. They rightly sense the Empire is rotten, and their plan goes as far as killing the Emperor and healing the world afterward.
- No group survives a Sentinel team at the door, and the Empire needs only one team once a target justifies the crossing. Cells stay small and get pruned, each loss passed down as a warning. The resistance can't win; it can only persist, teach, and keep the idea alive.

## The Bound Dead

Ember never fully leaves the dead, which is why there's anything to draw on and why the Vigil can bind its dead.

- The **lingering dead** are everywhere and harmless: residue that fades as ember scatters, felt by Listeners and sensed by others as a mood. Battlefields are thick with it.
- The **stuck dead** are rare and wrong. Sudden, unwilling, or emotion-soaked deaths can bind to a place or object on their own, keeping only the screaming part. Laying one to rest lets its ember scatter, a mercy and an exorcism.
- The **made dead** are corpses bound with ember like vessels and fueled from the ladder, a waste before they're a violation. Hollow constructs are mindless puppets; bound constructs, driven by a torn death, are walking wraith-blades. The craft is nearly lost, and one walking corpse can empty villages and bring Sentinels.
- The **willing dead** are the Vigil's sacrament: masters bound into blades or stones, rationed and slowly spent.
- The Emperor is a willing construct on a vast scale, a death that won't scatter, running on an artery into a dying god. The Church hunts bound dead as witchcraft while serving the only one that ever fully worked. With nothing to compare him to, nobody recognizes him.

## The Horrors

The usual fantasy bestiary exists. The dying adds a layer from inside the world, with no demon realm or invading evil, and new kinds keep appearing.

- **Wrong-beasts** are animals at the edges of the grays adapting to drain as well as eat: pack hunters that leave kills gray and uneaten, grazers that drain whatever bites them. They're the everyday frontier danger.
- **Kindled beasts** are animals born with the gift and no mind to restrain it. The classic case Quickens on instinct and keeps growing into a half-starved monster driven mad by its own hunger. Rare, without malice, and a local disaster when it happens.
- **Husks** are preserved dead in a deadzone moved by its pull, the land reaching toward the only warm life for miles. Graywalkers fear husk-thick stretches more than bare ground, which at least stays put.
- **The deliberate dead** are Turned work: raw-ember puppets, or worse, corpses with a torn death sealed back in, ended only by releasing the binding. When a predator's victims start rising, track down the maker.
- **Erdana's dreams** take form where the world is thinnest: nightmares with no biology, grief with just enough substance to touch you, draining warmth and filling places with dread. A Tender who meets one knows it's their god dreaming, and a dream can't be fought.
- **Erdana's fevers** are monsters she raises blindly against whatever is hurting her. They hunt the Kindled and can't tell a faithful Tender from a Turned or a Sentinel, so they attack those who might help her. They never form over Abira, where her core is too drained. A Tender hunted by one is hunted by what they've given their life to protect.
- In play, local heroes handle wrong-beasts, reputed heroes handle husks and risen dead, regional heroes hunt the Turned behind the deliberate dead, and Erdana's creations sit above all, encounters more than fights, best used sparingly. Most horrors come for ember because they're empty and you're full.

## Wylter the Eternal

- A man who wanted to live forever in a world with an end date, and reverse-engineered a civilization to do it quietly.
- Erdana had been worshiped as the goddess of life for so long that people forgot the world was her body. Wylter worked it out. Born a generation or two after the Vigil broke, when the Kindled were already distrusted, he built on both facts.
- As a young man he founded Erdana's Light, a new church around the old goddess, because tapping the heart required owning the ground above it and the story about it. Over a long natural life he made it the state faith and built an empire on hunting the Kindled who had spent the world, which gave him both a perimeter and an alibi. As an old man, with the Hallowseat finally over the Heart, he opened the artery two hundred years ago.
- He invented nothing. A sincere faith, a reasonable fear, and the Vigil's real crime all point the wrong way, which is why the lie holds.
- Everyone else drinks the runoff; he drinks from her heart. That is his deathlessness and his power, and the centuries left measure his theft of millennia.
- He is the valve. He opened the wound with rites only he knows, and only those rites close it. If he dies first, ember floods back, deserts bloom, the world drinks deep and celebrates, and in about ten years it empties completely and everything dies at once.
- Winning means taking him alive and extracting the rites first. Any faction that only wants him dead is an apocalypse cult without knowing it.
- Everyone who built his faith or understood him died of old age long ago. He is the last person alive who knows what the world is, and already lives as the sole survivor of a world that has forgotten it's dying. He thinks he's the only one with the nerve to refuse to die with everyone else.
- He rules from the Hallowseat in Abira, called the Heart, and the faithful kneel toward it as the seat of her favor.

## The Blackwater River

*Example frontier region.*

- A cold, fog-choked river running from dead country to the living sea, one of the last good routes through a dying frontier. Money and traffic cling to it, and so do predators.
- Danger follows it like temperature: deadly at the gray-locked head, contested in the middle reaches where the raiding happens, safe at the living mouth. Its dangerous stretches split into foggy tributaries full of ambushes and things from the gray. A crew that knows the tributaries owns the river.
- Settlements, head to mouth:
  - **Blackthorn Hold**, Varak's cliff fortress, reachable only by water he controls, where captives vanish into the dead country.
  - **Grayend**, the grimmest market town, existing by his allowance, where his cargo first sells.
  - **Sand Run**, a rough market town with fenced goods and holding pens.
  - **Riverport**, a trade town with some power of its own, quietly handling Varak's traffic too.
  - **Wavecrest**, the nicest and most ambitious middle town.
  - **Threshwater**, the great city at the mouth, built as a refuge for peoples the Empire discards and grown rich on their arrival. It's outward-facing, polyglot, and strong, and keeps an unspoken truce with Varak: too well defended to raid, unsure it could stop an armored Sentinel. Anyone fleeing him goes there.
- Varak preys on rich traffic in the middle reaches, gilded barges and noble convoys, and spares the small market towns that fence his goods and sell his captives. Robbing the river's rich and selling back into it keeps his operation stable.
- Threshwater, free of the throne and its informers and full of the Empire's discarded, is one of the resistance's best footholds. The network thickens there into a hub of shelter, money, and knowledge where Tenders rest and meet, its biggest strength and its biggest risk.
- Varak tolerates it out of hatred for the Empire, an ugly overlap of grudges nobody calls an alliance.
