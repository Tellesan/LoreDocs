# LYRIA
## Wessari Species Compendium

---

## Overview

The Wessari are a small and long-established people of the Luminari Empire, native to the cloud-shrouded forest world of Tayon'Wessar. Every Wessari lives in partnership with a Tayon, a symbiotic organism that bonds to the host in early childhood and holds their long-term memory for the rest of their life. The arrangement is older than the species' records. Freed from the work of remembering, the Wessari brain gives nearly all its capacity to perception, and the result is a people who notice almost everything and forget none of it.

They are few in number and spread thinly across the Empire with no strong pattern to where they settle or serve. Many citizens have never met one. Those who have usually came away with the sense of having been read closely by someone who never seemed to be looking.

---

## Appearance

The Wessari are tall and long-limbed, with a dancer's economy of movement. The sexes are clearly distinct. Males run larger and heavier, with more muscle across the shoulders and back and larger frills. Females are smaller and finer-boned, with smaller frills and far richer coloring, their banding brighter and more varied than any male's.

The skin is smooth and faintly scaled, carrying a soft sheen in muted base tones, with darker or brighter banding across the cheekbones and down the sides of the throat. The pattern of the banding is individual and settles fully in adolescence. The face is plated, smooth hardened scale running across the brow, the cheekbones, and down the line of the jaw, with a flat nose bridge and slit nostrils. The skull is a rounded dome of overlapping plates. The frills are a set of low, swept-back fins tucked close against the back of the skull, and they move with mood, lifting slightly in interest or alarm and lying flat in calm. Their size and shape vary between individuals, and some carry broader frills that flare toward the jaw. They have no hair.

The eyes are large and dark, with little white showing, and reflective in low light the way a cat's are. They evolved for a world where the sun never fully reaches the ground, and most Wessari find other planets glaring. Tinted lenses are common off-world, and Wessari homes and offices tend to be kept dim.

The hands have five long fingers with finely textured pads, a grip adaptation left over from life in the trees. Each finger carries a retractable claw, sheathed along the fingertip and extended at will.

The Tayon lies along the back of the neck and down the spine as a raised, ridged line beneath the skin. When it is working, in deep recall, in strong emotion, or when a scent pulls a memory loose, it glows faintly through the skin.

Seeing the Tayon is intimate, and Wessari clothing is cut to cover it. High collars, wrapped necklines, and hoods run through every Wessari fashion, formal or casual. A lowered collar, a bared nape, or a hand resting at the back of someone's neck all carry meaning among Wessari. A glow showing at the edge of a collar in company is noticed and politely ignored, the way one might ignore a blush. Outsiders rarely understand any of this, and a Wessari who wears an open collar among other species has usually decided that no one present can read it.

Since the Tayon holds everything a Wessari is, armor over the back of the neck is common. A hard nape guard, worn under a high collar or built into it, is standard for any Wessari in danger and ordinary for many who are not. In formal dress the guard survives as a decorative gorget, often worked with family designs.

Their faces are mobile and expressive among their own kind. In public, especially in Imperial service, many hold them still.

## Biology

### Body

The Wessari run cooler than humans and feel the cold sharply. Warmth draws them, and they keep their homes warm as well as dim, seek out heated rooms and sunlit corners off-world, and grow slow and irritable in cold places. They do not sweat.

Wessari muscle is denser than a human's. They look lean and move lightly, a legacy of climbing in the middle reaches, but they carry more strength than their frames suggest, and anyone who takes hold of one expecting a slight build learns otherwise.

They are mainly carnivorous, with teeth and digestion built for meat. Four long, slender fangs sit at the front of the mouth, two upper and two lower, sharp enough to pierce scale and set slightly forward of the other teeth. At rest the lips cover them. They show when a Wessari speaks with feeling, laughs openly, or draws air across the palate to read a room, which is part of why the habit unsettles other species. Their lives run somewhat longer than a human's, which still leaves a Tayon outliving four or five of them.

### Venom

The Wessari are venomous. They can release a toxin at will through the fine gaps between their scales, and they control its strength. In mild doses it acts as a light hallucinogen and an aphrodisiac. In concentrated doses it is a potent nerve toxin, enough to kill most species on contact with skin or blood.

A diluted form is always present in their bodily fluids, including saliva and seed. Intimacy with a Wessari is chemically charged for other species, a mild haze of heightened sensation and want, whether the Wessari intends it or not. The faint presence of the toxin may also be part of why other species so often find the Wessari either unsettling or strangely compelling.

The Wessari themselves are resistant. A dose that would kill another species produces only the aphrodisiac effect in them, and the toxin plays a natural part in mating between Wessari.

Since they do not sweat, the toxin takes that role under stress. Strong emotion draws it to the skin in its mild form without any choice on the Wessari's part, and fear draws it most of all, as a defense. A frightened Wessari carries a thin film of it across the skin, and anything that bites or grabs them takes in a dose. Among themselves this is also how stress reads. Another Wessari can taste fear on the air from across a room, so the mild leak is hard to hide from their own kind, even when the face gives nothing away.

### The Tayon

The Tayon is a separate organism that lives coiled at the base of the skull and along the upper spine, threaded into the host's nervous system. It records everything the host perceives at full fidelity, including scent, and holds it for life. The host brain retains short-term memory, instinct, and the body's learned skills. Everything else lives in the Tayon.

Ordinary recall is quick and effortless. Deep recall is something else. The host goes still, the eyes unfocus, the Tayon glows, and the memory arrives whole. For a few seconds they are inside it again, smelling what they smelled and feeling what they felt. Strong memories can surface unbidden, most often when a scent triggers them, and Wessari learn early to ride these out without letting them show.

A Tayon lives far longer than any host, long enough to pass through four or five of them across its life. It carries the memories of every host it has lived in, and those memories surface for the current host in the same way their own do, though older lives come less readily and with less clarity.

### The Tayon's Nature

The Tayon is semi-aware. It has moods, preferences, and something close to a personality, but it cannot speak or think alongside its host in any way that resembles conversation. It communicates only through flashes of memory. A Tayon uneasy around a stranger might surface a former host's memory of betrayal. One that has taken to someone might offer up an old, warm moment with no bearing on the present.

Hosts learn to read these flashes the way one learns to read a companion animal, and living with a Tayon is a lifelong relationship with someone who can never quite be talked to. Some Wessari trust the flashes absolutely and treat the Tayon's moods as a second judgment. Others regard them as noise and train themselves to set them aside. Most fall somewhere between, and a Wessari's attitude toward their Tayon says a good deal about them.

### Senses

The Wessari evolved in the middle reaches of a dim, layered forest, where light was poor, sound carried badly through wet air, and predators came from above and below. Two senses carried them through it.

The first is scent. A vomeronasal organ runs along the roof of the mouth, separate from the ordinary sense of smell. To read a person or a room closely, a Wessari parts their lips slightly and draws air across the palate. Through it they pick up fear, arousal, illness, fatigue, grief, and the sour edge that deceit leaves in sweat. The nose handles the world. The palate handles people. Other species tend to find the habit either unsettling or oddly intimate, and Wessari in mixed company often learn to do it discreetly.

The second is sight. Wessari visual processing runs faster than most species', catching movements and expressions too brief for others to register. A micro-expression lasting a fraction of a second, a shift of weight, a hand tightening, or a breath held a beat too long all arrive clearly.

Combined, and backed by a Tayon that remembers how every person they have ever met looked and smelled when calm, the two senses let a Wessari read another person's state with an accuracy that others often mistake for mind-reading. It works entirely through anatomy and attention, with no help from the Weave.

### Sharing Memory

A Wessari can share a memory with another person through touch, and only at intimate range. Between two Wessari, it means laying the fingers against the back of the other's neck, over their Tayon. With another species the contact is similar, fingers at the nape or the base of the skull. The memory passes as recall, arriving in flashes of vision, sound, and scent, so that the receiver sees and smells what the sharer once did.

Only the host can choose to share. Nothing can be drawn out of a Tayon against its host's will, and the act requires both trust and closeness, which keeps it rare. Among Wessari it is a gesture of deep confidence, used between partners, within families, and occasionally to settle a dispute by showing exactly what happened. Sharing a memory with someone of another species is rarer still, and most Wessari will go their whole lives without doing it.

### Biting and Blood

Blood carries everything a Wessari's palate reads, and more of it. A taste of someone's blood gives up their health, their fear or calm, their arousal, and the chemical signature that makes them who they are, all at a strength the air could never carry. The Tayon keeps that taste for life, and for every life after.

Biting someone and tasting their blood is among the most intimate acts a Wessari can offer or ask for. Between Wessari it happens between partners and lovers, most often during mating, usually at the throat or shoulder, and it leaves small paired marks the bitten one tends to wear without covering. Letting someone bite you means letting them know you completely and keep that knowledge in their Tayon long after you are gone. Biting without consent is an assault of the worst kind, and biting a stranger in a fight is something Wessari remember for generations.

Saliva carries the diluted toxin, so a bite always delivers a small dose. Another Wessari, resistant, feels only a flush of the aphrodisiac. A partner of another species feels the same warm haze as from any other contact, concentrated at the wound, and a Wessari who bites an outsider has to judge the dose the same way they would during mating.

Few outsiders understand what the bite means. A Wessari who asks to taste someone's blood is asking for far more than the other person usually realizes, and most Wessari who take lovers from other species tell them first.

### Bonding

A Wessari child without a Tayon lives on instinct and muscle memory. They learn to move, to feed, and to recognize their family, but almost nothing lasts. When the child begins to walk, the family knows they are ready, and the bonding follows soon after.

Most Wessari have no true memories from before their bonding, only a sense of their own body and a few feelings without context. The first clear memory a Wessari carries is often their Tayon's rather than their own, a moment from a former host's life or, for a fresh spawn, a blurred fragment of something inherited.

The window for bonding closes in early childhood. A Wessari who passes it without a Tayon, or who loses theirs later, cannot bond again.

### Inheritance

When a host dies, their Tayon passes to a new one, unless it judges itself too old to take another host. Since only young children can bond, the recipient is always a child at or near walking age. Tayon usually pass down family lines, but the choice belongs to the host, who names their heir in life. The heir can come from outside the family, and leaving one's Tayon to another family's child creates a bond between the two households that can last generations. Passing over one's own descendants is a statement everyone notices.

A host who reaches middle age without naming a recipient is considered careless. If a host dies without one, the family decides who receives the Tayon.

### The Tayon as Teacher

A Tayon passed from an older host to a young child often takes on the role of teacher. It surfaces memories deliberately, offering the child a former host learning to read, handling a tool, settling an argument, or keeping still while something hunted nearby, and it offers them when the child needs them. Over the years the Tayon guides as much as it remembers, steering its new host with the experience of the ones before.

Children raised this way learn quickly and unevenly, far ahead of their age in whatever their Tayon thinks worth teaching and ordinary everywhere else. They tend to seem older than their years, and sometimes stranger. Families pay attention to which lessons a Tayon chooses, since the choice reflects the hosts it has known and what they valued.

A fresh spawn has no lives of its own to draw on, only the blurred inheritance from its parent, and the child who bonds with one learns more from their family than from their Tayon.

### Spawning and Names

A Tayon decides for itself when its time has come. When its host dies and it knows it is too old to find another, it does not pass on. It is carried home to Tayon'Wessar, to the spawning pools, where it produces young and dies. A host may name an heir in life only to find that, at their death, the Tayon has chosen the pools instead.

The pools are among the most closely guarded places on the homeworld, and everything about them is ritualized, from the journey home with the Tayon to the spawning itself. The loss of an old Tayon is mourned as the end of every life it carried, and the spawn are received as their continuation.

The spawn carry their parent's memories genetically, imperfectly, a blurred inheritance of half-remembered lives rather than the sharp recall of a mature Tayon. Every Tayon begins life with some sense of the lives before it.

Each spawn is named by its parent, and the name comes through to the host in early memory flashes. Once the child is old enough to speak, they know their full name and can tell others.

### Reproduction and Population

The Wessari bear live young, and multiple births are common.

Every Wessari child needs a Tayon by the time they walk, and the supply is finite. A Tayon comes free only when its host dies, and new spawn come only when an old Tayon goes to the pools. Multiple births make the pressure sharper, since twins or triplets need two or three Tayon at once. The species' birth rate is tied to its Tayon supply, and families plan children around deaths and spawnings with long, careful patience. A family that has children without a Tayon waiting for each of them is gambling that enough will come free in time. A child who reaches walking age with none is a tragedy, and the reason the population stays small. Siblings from the same birth who receive Tayon of very different ages, one a fresh spawn and one carrying five lives, often grow up as very different people.

### Sexual Anatomy

At rest, neither sex shows much. A Wessari's sexual anatomy sits concealed low on the groin, behind skin that is finer and softer than the surrounding scale but otherwise blends into it. Like the Tayon, it stays covered, and outsiders often have no idea what lies there until they find out.

#### Males

The male carries a single sheath, a fold of soft, fine-scaled skin. When aroused, two shafts emerge from it side by side. Each is humanoid in overall shape, with a defined head and a straight or gently curved length, and each is larger than a human's, in proportion to the male's heavier frame. The coloring follows the individual's banding, so the pattern matches the one on his face and throat and is as personal as either.

Each shaft is studded. Small, rounded studs are scattered across its length, sparse near the head and growing denser toward the base. At rest they are barely raised, little more than a texture under the skin. With arousal they fill and firm, standing out clearly, a shade darker than the skin around them.

Each stud is a gland, with a fine pore at its tip. During mating the studs release the toxin in its mild form, deliberately, to bring a partner to arousal, and its effect begins on contact. The dose comes from the same points that grip, so it rises as the male's arousal does.

Ordinarily only one shaft is used at a time. A male has some control over which, and over whether the second emerges at all. Mating among Wessari has long since become as much about pleasure as reproduction, and some take pleasure in using both at once.

At climax the shaft swells and the studs swell with it, full and firm, densest at the base where they hold.

#### Females

The female anatomy sits behind a single external slit, which opens into two closely paired internal channels matching the male's pairing. One channel is used ordinarily. Both can be used together.

The skin around the slit carries the brightest part of the female's banding, rarely seen. Inside, the walls are lined with soft ridges. During mating they grip and texture, and the male's studs catch between them. At her climax the ridges tighten around the swollen studs, and together with his swelling this holds the pair joined in a knot that lasts until both have settled and the swelling eases. The lock evolved to keep the pair together long enough for conception, and among Wessari it is one of the most intimate parts of mating.

Live birth of multiples shapes the rest of her. Females are smaller and finer than males, but their hips run wider than their slim build suggests, and the internal anatomy is very elastic, which accommodates both the male's size and the occasional use of both channels at once.

Females release no toxin internally. During mating they release it externally, through the skin wherever they are pressed against their mate, so both partners dose each other.

With arousal, a female's banding brightens across the face and throat and the colors deepen. Any Wessari can read it at a glance. Women in public and in Imperial service often learn to hold the color down, the same way they learn to keep their faces still, and a flush of brightened banding in a formal setting is noticed and politely ignored, like a glow at the edge of a collar.

### Mating

Mating between Wessari is shaped by the toxin. Both partners release it at full strength, and with their resistance it works purely as an aphrodisiac. Each partner's release heightens the other's, so arousal builds on itself, and Wessari mating tends to be long and intense. It ends in the lock, the pair held together through the aftermath, which Wessari regard as the closest physical bond two people can share short of sharing a memory.

A partner from another species receives the toxin only in its mild form, and the haze described under Venom deepens with exposure and takes on a light hallucinogenic edge. A Wessari who wants a partner from another species has to be deliberate about the dose, since the strength that is ordinary between Wessari would kill them. Most Wessari treat that control as one of the first responsibilities of intimacy with outsiders.

Other species lack the matching ridges, but the lock can still form. The male's swelling at climax holds the partner, and the toxin released from his studs draws an involuntary clenching from their internal muscles, centered on the very points that grip, much as a Wessari female's ridges would. The partner has no control over it. How firmly they lock depends on the dose, how long they have been exposed, and how their species reacts, and with enough of all three it can hold nearly as firmly as between two Wessari. For the partner it is overwhelming, and the Wessari carries the responsibility for it. A careless dose can leave a partner locked far longer and far harder than they bargained for.

### Hybrids

The Wessari are partially cross-fertile with some other species. Hybrid offspring lack several of the features that make the Wessari distinct and cannot bond with a Tayon. Their memory comes from the other parent's side and works in the ordinary way.

The Wessari side still shows. Hybrids tend to be impulsive and instinctive, quick to act and slow to settle, noticing everything and holding still for very little. Among the Wessari this reads as familiar, the restlessness of a toddler before bonding, and it tends to draw fondness rather than impatience.

Hybrids are rare and are welcomed as family without reservation. Since they never need a Tayon, they also never live under the pressure of waiting for one.

### Loss

Losing a Tayon is permanent amnesia. The host survives, keeping their skills, their instincts, and their short-term memory, but everything they were goes with the Tayon. They cannot bond again.

The memories a Tayon holds can be reached only through its host, and removing it destroys them. A Wessari's knowledge can be erased along with who they were, but no one can take it.

## Tayon'Wessar

The homeworld lies under permanent cloud. Sunlight reaches the ground as a dim green haze filtered through tier after tier of forest canopy, and the world has never known a clear day. The forest runs in layers, the high canopy, the middle reaches, and the dark floor, each holding its own ecosystem. The Wessari evolved in the middle reaches, where survival meant noticing a leaf moving against the wind, a sour note in the air, or a silence where there should have been birdsong.

The name joins the two species that share the world, the Tayon and the Wessari, in the same order a Wessari's own name joins them.

Wessari cities are built into the middle reaches, broad platforms and enclosed halls grown and woven into the great trees, lit low and kept warm. They are quiet places. Visitors from brighter worlds find them hard to see in, and the hush of the halls, where most conversation happens at close range and low voice, takes some getting used to.

The spawning pools lie somewhere below the cities, and their exact locations are not shared with outsiders. Every Wessari, wherever in the Empire they live, knows that their Tayon will make that journey home one day, carried by someone else.

## Society

### Names

Every Wessari carries a compound name, the Tayon's name first and the host's own name second. The Tayon's half stays with it from host to host, so a full name tells another Wessari which lineage of memory a person holds and, often, whose life they inherited.

The full name is used only among Wessari. Outsiders know a Wessari by the single, personal name, and giving the full name to someone from another species is a rare and significant act, close in meaning to letting them see the Tayon.

Wessari rarely speak of their Tayon at all, even among friends. Whose lives it carries, what it shows them, and how they get along with it stay private unless the knowledge is needed or useful. Asking a Wessari directly about their Tayon is a mild rudeness they will usually forgive in outsiders and rarely in their own kind.

### Family

Wessari family life runs on long timescales. Births are planned around the deaths and spawnings that free Tayon, heirs are named years or decades in advance, and the movement of Tayon between households builds ties between families that outlast any individual in them. A Wessari family knows its own history in detail, since several of its members carry parts of it firsthand.

Families are close-knit and selective. They are careful about whom they marry into, whom they name as heirs from outside, and whom they let near their affairs at all. The same care makes them formidable. A family whose Tayon carry generations of experience in law, trade, or administration raises children who start their careers already knowing how the work is done and how it went wrong last time, and those families tend to hold standing well above their numbers.

## Behavior

Wessari are attentive in a way other species feel before they can name it. In conversation they watch closely, sometimes too closely, and they remember everything said. A Wessari who repeats someone's words back to them weeks later will have them exactly right, along with how the speaker smelled when they said them.

They tend toward patience, a product of family lives built around waiting and of minds that do not lose the details of a problem while they consider it. They are slow to trust, having watched too many people say one thing while their bodies said another.

Among their own kind they are physically close and warm. The stillness many keep in public serves them well in Imperial service and can make them seem cold to people who never see them at home.

## Relations

### Luminari Empire

The Wessari are long-standing members of the Luminari Empire. Families have served it for generations, and old Tayon carry firsthand memory of its earlier eras. A Wessari in the room may hold the memory of a treaty's original terms, the exact words of a long-dead official, or the last time a dispute like the present one came up.

Wherever they settle, Wessari families tend to hold high standing. Their members make exceptional administrators and officials, precise, patient, and very hard to mislead, and the Empire has learned to value them accordingly. Their population is too small for them to dominate any institution, but a Wessari name turns up in senior posts far more often than their numbers would suggest.
